The JLG 450AJ is a 45 ft diesel articulating boom lift with a jib, four-wheel drive, and oscillating axles. It is one of the most common machines in North American rental fleets because 45 ft covers most commercial construction and industrial maintenance, the articulating boom reaches up and over obstacles that a straight boom cannot, and the chassis handles rough ground.

The 450AJ HC3 is the high-capacity version of the same chassis with a 1,000 lb platform rating. The E450AJ is the electric counterpart with a narrower chassis for indoor use.

## Series II vs current production

Series II machines (roughly 2004–2019) carry a 500 lb platform rating and a slightly shorter published outreach (about 24 ft 8 in). Current-production units moved to a 550 lb rating, a redesigned hood JLG calls DuraTough, and updated platform control electronics. Parts interchange within a series is good; across series, confirm by serial number before ordering control-system components.

## What fails on rental-fleet 450AJs

Rental hours on a 450AJ concentrate wear in a predictable set of components:

- **Platform control box and joysticks.** The platform console takes the most abuse of anything on the machine. Drive/steer and boom joysticks are the usual replacement.
- **Tilt and level sensors.** The chassis tilt alarm and the platform level system depend on them; intermittent tilt faults after a wash-down are almost always a sensor or its connector.
- **Foot switch and E-stops.** Cheap, frequently replaced, and the cause of many "no function from platform" complaints.
- **Solenoids on the main valve** for boom, drive, and steer. Sticking solenoids show up as one function lagging while the rest work.
- **Alarms, horn, and strobe.** Required for compliance and routinely broken.
- **Engine service items**: filters, belts, glow plugs, and the fuel shutoff solenoid.

## Operating notes

The 450AJ is Group B, Type 3 under ANSI A92: it travels elevated with controls in the platform, and the platform extends past the tipping line. Operators need boom-lift training, familiarization on the specific unit, and a harness attached to the platform anchor at all times. See [MEWP training requirements](/insights/mobile-elevating-work-platform-training).

Weight is roughly 14,500–15,000 lb, so it moves by flatbed or tilt-deck trailer, not a pickup. Foam-filled tires add weight but eliminate flats on job sites.

## Frequently asked questions

**How high does a JLG 450AJ reach?**
45 ft platform height, about 51 ft working height, with 25 ft of horizontal outreach on current units.

**What is the platform capacity of a 450AJ?**
550 lb on current production; 500 lb on Series II units. The 450AJ HC3 variant is rated 1,000 lb.

**Is the 450AJ four-wheel drive?**
Yes, with 45% gradeability and oscillating axles for rough terrain.

**What engine is in a JLG 450AJ?**
Diesel; Deutz on many Series II builds and Kubota on many current builds. Check the engine tag, since parts differ.

**What is the difference between a 450A and 450AJ?**
The J indicates a jib on the end of the boom, which adds articulation for positioning the platform around obstacles. The 450A has no jib.
