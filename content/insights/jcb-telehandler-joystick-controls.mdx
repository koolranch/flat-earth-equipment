A JCB telehandler's joystick is the single point of contact for lifting, extending, and placing a load — so when it feels off, safety and productivity both take a hit. This guide covers what the stick actually controls, the checks worth running before you assume it's dead, and the fitment details that matter if you do need a replacement.

## What the joystick controls

Most JCB telehandlers use a single, fully proportional, seat-mounted servo joystick to run the core boom functions:

- **Boom lift and lower** — raises and lowers the boom
- **Boom extend and retract** — telescopes the boom in and out
- **Crowd and dump (tilt)** — angles the forks or attachment for loading and placing
- **Auxiliary hydraulics** — runs attachments like grapples, buckets, or sweepers via a rocker switch or roller on the grip

The joystick only arms when the seat switch is engaged, which is why an operator sitting down but getting no response is a common first symptom rather than a coincidence. Steering is handled separately, usually with a column-mounted mode selector for front-wheel, all-wheel, and crab steering — worth knowing when you're troubleshooting so you don't chase a steering fault through the boom joystick.

## When the stick feels wrong

Before ordering a replacement, run through these checks — most "dead joystick" complaints trace back to something upstream of the stick itself:

- **No response at all** — confirm the seat switch is engaging (a worn or misaligned switch reads as "operator not seated" even with someone in the seat) and check the multi-pin connector at the base of the joystick for corrosion or a loose latch.
- **Intermittent buttons or aux function** — trace the harness from the joystick base down through the seat riser for chafe points; constant seat travel over years of use is a common wear spot.
- **One function works, others don't** — points to a specific switch or pot inside the joystick rather than the harness, which usually means the stick itself needs replacing.
- **Drift or self-creeping boom movement** — check the centering spring and pivot before condemning the joystick; a bent or worn pivot can mimic an electrical fault.
- **Hydraulics won't enable** — confirm the enable/deadman circuit (seat switch, park brake interlock, or a dedicated enable button depending on model) before assuming the joystick has failed.

If you've ruled out the connector, harness, and seat switch and the fault stays with the stick, it's time to look at a replacement.

## LH vs RH and fitment

JCB telehandlers use both left-hand and right-hand joystick assemblies depending on cab layout and function count, and they are not interchangeable. Before ordering:

- Confirm **LH or RH** mounting position in your cab
- Match the **function count** (number of buttons/rockers) to your current stick — extra aux circuits mean extra switches
- Verify against your **model and serial number**, since function layout has changed across production runs

Aftermarket joysticks are a solid option once you've confirmed fitment — eligible aftermarket JCB parts carry a 2-year warranty, so you're not trading reliability for savings.

## Cab companions: seats and forks

The joystick doesn't work in isolation — a worn [suspension seat](/parts/jcb-400-h9799-suspension-seat-vinyl) changes how consistently an operator can reach and modulate the stick over a full shift, and a straight, properly matched [fork](/parts/333C3422) means the precision you're dialing in at the joystick actually shows up at the load. Both are common companions to check when you're already in the cab replacing controls.
